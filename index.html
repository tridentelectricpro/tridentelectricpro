Planning an EV charger

Level 2 charging typically uses a dedicated 240-volt circuit. The right circuit size depends on the charger, the vehicle and the spare capacity in your panel, which is why a load check comes before installation. Sometimes a panel upgrade is part of the job.

Electrical Safety

Do not try to diagnose or repair hazardous electrical work yourself

Electrical systems can involve energized conductors, service equipment, fault current and other hazards. If you see burning, sparking, smoke, repeated breaker trips, damaged wiring or another potentially dangerous condition, avoid opening equipment or attempting a repair yourself. In an emergency such as fire, call 911.

A qualified electrical professional can assess the situation using appropriate test equipment, protective equipment and safe work procedures.

Bellevue Climate & Electrical Considerations

How local weather can affect electrical planning

Western Washington has a mild marine climate with a wetter cool season and a generally drier summer pattern. Seattle-area National Weather Service climate information shows substantially more precipitation in the cooler months than in July and August. See National Weather Service information.

Wet-season conditions

Rain and moisture make outdoor electrical equipment, exterior lighting, receptacles and other exposed systems important considerations. Electrical work should account for the equipment's intended location and protection requirements.

Winter outages and backup power

Homeowners and businesses may consider generator and backup-power planning where continuity of power matters. See generator services.

Summer electrical projects

What should I do if an outlet is sparking or wiring appears damaged?

Do not attempt to open or repair energized electrical equipment yourself. If there is immediate danger such as smoke or fire, follow emergency procedures and call 911. For electrical service, contact a qualified professional.

How do I know if I need an electrical panel upgrade?

Common signs include breakers that trip often, lights that dim when large appliances start, a fuse box or an older panel, no open breaker spaces, or plans to add an EV charger, heat pump, hot tub or home addition. A panel scorched by heat, buzzing or smelling of burning is a safety concern that needs prompt attention from a qualified electrician.
