What should I do during a power outage to stay safe?

Unplug sensitive electronics to protect them from surges when power returns, and keep the refrigerator and freezer closed to hold the cold. Use flashlights instead of candles, and never run a generator indoors or in a garage, since the exhaust can be deadly. If you see sparking, damage, or a burning smell, stay clear and call an electrician.

Is aluminum wiring in an older home a safety concern?

Aluminum wiring was common in homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, and it expands and loosens at connections more than copper does, which can cause overheating at outlets and switches. It does not always mean rewiring, since approved repair methods can make connections safe. An inspection can tell you whether your home has it and what to do.

About Honolulu

Electrical work in Honolulu

Honolulu's tropical climate shapes its electrical work more than almost anywhere else in the country. Salt air corrodes outdoor panels, meter bases, and exposed connections faster than on the mainland, and the combination of heat and humidity is hard on wiring insulation and on equipment in attics and garages. Many homes in Honolulu were built in the postwar decades, and their electrical systems were not designed for today's air conditioning loads, which run for much of the year. Air conditioning circuits, pool and lanai electrical, and outdoor lighting all see heavy use, and a panel that cannot keep up will show it with tripping breakers and warm outlets. Solar is widespread across Oahu, which adds its own layer: inverter and disconnect work, and panels that need to be ready for a solar tie-in. If your Honolulu home's panel is original, shows corrosion, or trips when the air conditioning runs, it is worth having the system checked.

Commercial electrical work in Honolulu

Commercial electrical work runs on a different clock from residential work, because a business in Honolulu cannot simply shut down while we figure things out. Restaurants adding kitchen equipment, offices rearranging workspaces, and retail shops refreshing their lighting all need power work that fits around opening hours. We start by learning how the space is used and what the new layout demands, then we plan the circuits, panels, and lighting around it. On site in Hawaii, we coordinate with the owner or manager, keep the work area safe and tidy, and test each circuit as we finish it, so the business can get back to serving its customers.

Across Hawaii

Electrical problems we hear about most across Hawaii

Hawaii's salt air is the biggest enemy of electrical equipment, corroding panels, connections, and outdoor fixtures far faster than on the mainland. Older plantation-era and postwar homes often have small panels and few circuits, which become a problem when solar, battery storage, or an EV charger is added. Storm outages test backup plans, and humidity causes outdoor outlets and lighting to fail early. If a panel shows rust or breakers trip when the air conditioner runs, have it checked before adding new loads.
