Quick answer

What is electrical repair and troubleshooting?

Electrical repair is the diagnosis and correction of an existing electrical fault, such as a breaker that keeps tripping, a dead or warm outlet, flickering lights or lost power. A licensed electrician tests the circuit to find the cause, repairs or replaces the failed part, and verifies the fix before leaving. Trident Electric does this for homes, businesses and multifamily properties from Bellevue, WA.

  • Burning smells, heat, buzzing or arcing are urgent. Call 911 for fire or smoke.
  • Finding the cause first avoids replacing parts at random.

Tripping breakers

Why does a circuit breaker keep tripping?

A breaker trips to protect the wiring. The pattern of tripping points to the cause: instantly on reset suggests a short or ground fault, while tripping after minutes of use suggests overload or heat.

Overloaded circuit

Too much load on one circuit heats the wire, so the breaker trips after a few minutes of use. Common with space heaters, hair dryers and kitchen appliances. The fix may be moving loads or adding a dedicated circuit.

Short circuit

A hot conductor touches a neutral or ground conductor, causing a sudden surge. The breaker usually trips instantly, sometimes with a pop or scorch mark. This needs a licensed electrician.

Ground fault

Current escapes along an unintended path, often from moisture or a damaged appliance. GFCI outlets and breakers trip to prevent shock, so repeated trips mean the source must be found.

Arc fault

Damaged cable, a loose wire connection or a nail through a wire can arc and overheat. AFCI breakers trip on this, most often on bedroom and living-area circuits.

Worn or faulty breaker

A breaker can trip below its rating or fail to hold after years of use or heat. Test the circuit first, because replacing the breaker will not fix a fault in the wiring.

Failing connection at the panel

A loose or heat-damaged connection can trip breakers at random or cause flickering throughout the home. This is panel work that should only be handled by a professional.

Safety

What is safe to check, and when to stop

Safe to check first

  • Unplug devices on the affected circuit, then reset the breaker once
  • Press RESET on GFCI outlets in kitchens, baths, garages and outdoors
  • Note when it happens, which room, and what was running
  • Take photos of anything affected, only if it is safe to approach

Stop and call a professional

  • The breaker trips again after one reset
  • An outlet, switch or panel is warm, buzzing or discolored
  • You smell burning or see scorch marks
  • You would need to open the panel or touch wiring
Emergency: if you see fire, smoke or arcing, leave the area and call 911. For a hot or buzzing panel or water near electrical equipment, stay away and call a qualified electrician. Switch off a circuit only if it is safe to reach.

Cost and permits

What affects the price of an electrical repair

Repair cost is driven by the nature of the fault and how much work it takes to reach and fix it. Many repairs need an on-site assessment before a scope and price can be set, because the cause is not visible until the circuit is tested.

  • How easily the wiring and panel can be reached
  • The condition and capacity of existing wiring and the panel
  • Parts and materials required
  • Permit and inspection requirements for the scope
  • Whether the fault is constant or intermittent

Repair, rewire or upgrade?

A single failed device or loose connection is usually a targeted repair. Repeated failures across a house, two-prong outlets, aluminum or deteriorated wiring may point to rewiring. A full, outdated or overheating panel may call for a panel upgrade. Permit rules depend on the work and the jurisdiction. In Washington, the state publishes electrical rules at lni.wa.gov.

FAQ

Electrical repair questions, answered

Why does my breaker keep tripping?

A breaker keeps tripping because of an overloaded circuit, a short circuit, a ground fault, an arc fault, or a worn breaker. If it trips right after you reset it with nothing plugged in, the fault is likely in the wiring and needs a licensed electrician to locate it.

Is it safe to keep resetting a tripped breaker?

No. A breaker trips to stop dangerous heat or current. Reset it once after unplugging devices on that circuit, and if it trips again, leave it off and call an electrician instead of resetting it repeatedly.

Why are my lights flickering?

Flickering usually comes from a loose bulb or fixture connection, a dimmer that is incompatible with the bulbs, a loose connection in a switch or the panel, or a heavy appliance starting on the same circuit. Flickering throughout the home, or with a burning smell, should be inspected promptly.

Why does an outlet have no power when the breaker has not tripped?

A tripped GFCI outlet elsewhere on the circuit, a loose connection behind the outlet, a failed device or a broken wire are the usual causes. Press RESET on nearby GFCI outlets (kitchen, bath, garage, exterior); if the outlet is still dead, have it traced.

Can I replace a breaker or outlet myself?

Breaker replacement involves working inside the electrical panel, where parts stay energized, so it should be left to a qualified professional. Outlet and switch replacement is also best handled by a licensed electrician, especially if you see damage, heat, or aluminum or older wiring.

How much does electrical repair cost?

Cost depends on the scope of the fault, how hard the wiring is to reach, the condition and capacity of the existing wiring and panel, the parts needed, and permit and inspection requirements. Many repairs need an on-site assessment before a scope and price can be given.

Do electrical repairs need a permit?

It depends on the work and the jurisdiction. Replacing a failed device is often treated differently from adding circuits or changing a panel, which commonly need a permit and inspection. We explain what applies to your project before work starts.

How long does an electrical repair take?

A like-for-like device replacement can be quick. A hidden fault, such as a short inside a wall or an intermittent connection, takes longer because the circuit has to be traced and tested before the repair.

What should I do in an electrical emergency?

If you see fire, smoke, or arcing, leave the area and call 911. For a burning smell, a hot or buzzing panel, or water near electrical equipment, switch off the affected circuit only if it is safe to reach, stay away from the area, and call a qualified electrician.
