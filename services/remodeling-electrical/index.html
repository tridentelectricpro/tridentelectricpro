Direct Answer

What is remodeling electrical work?

Remodeling electrical is the wiring, circuit, lighting, and panel work needed to support a renovation. It includes relocating or adding outlets and switches, running new dedicated circuits for appliances, installing lighting, adding GFCI/AFCI protection, upgrading panels, and correcting hidden wiring problems found when walls are opened. Trident Electric provides this for kitchens, bathrooms, basements, additions, and whole-home renovations.

Best fit: any project that changes a floor plan, adds appliances or equipment, or adds finished living space.

Service Scope

Electrical work built around your renovation

A remodel changes how a space uses power. A new island, a walk-in shower, a finished basement, or a relocated laundry room each adds loads that the original wiring was never designed for. Trident Electric reviews your plan, checks the existing system, and designs circuits and devices to match the new layout.

Kitchens and bathrooms add moisture and high-load appliances, so protection, circuit sizing, and fixture ratings matter. Older homes may also hide ungrounded circuits, aluminum branch wiring, or overloaded panels that are best corrected while the walls are open.

Requirements, permits, and licensing vary by jurisdiction. Use the service-area directory to find your city and state page.

Signs your renovation needs electrical work

Changing a kitchen layout

New appliance locations and countertops usually need new circuits and receptacle placement.

Adding recessed lighting

Finishing a basement

Finished space needs planned circuits, outlets, lighting, and often heat.

Moving appliances

Opening walls

Exposed walls are the best time to correct or upgrade wiring.

Old wiring discovered

Cloth-covered, aluminum, or ungrounded wiring should be evaluated before it is covered.

Breakers tripping

New loads on old circuits can overload them. A load review identifies the fix.

Adding heated floors or a sauna

High-load features need dedicated, correctly sized circuits.

Selling or insuring

Inspectors and insurers may flag outdated panels or wiring that remodeling can resolve.

Cost Factors

What affects remodeling electrical cost

No two remodels are alike, so pricing depends on scope rather than a flat rate. The biggest drivers are:

  • Number of new or relocated circuits
  • Wall and ceiling access
  • Panel capacity and available breaker spaces
  • Count of fixtures, switches, and receptacles
  • Wet-location and specialty requirements
  • Age and condition of existing wiring
  • Permits and inspections
Permits & Code

Permits, inspections, and code

New circuits, rewiring, and panel work typically require an electrical permit and inspection. Many kitchens, bathrooms, basements, and laundry areas also require GFCI and AFCI protection, but the exact rules depend on the code edition your jurisdiction has adopted.

Tip: Ask for a written estimate that lists circuits, devices, and permit handling so you can compare bids fairly.

Safety First

Do not DIY hazardous electrical work

Avoid concealing connections or modifying wiring without understanding the existing circuit. Remodeling can uncover older or altered work that needs professional evaluation before it is covered.

For faults, panel work, new circuits, high-load equipment, wet locations, or concealed wiring, call a qualified electrician.

FAQ

Remodeling electrical: frequently asked questions

Can you handle electrical work during a kitchen remodel?

Yes. Kitchen remodels commonly involve new or relocated appliance circuits, countertop receptacles, GFCI and AFCI protection, under-cabinet and recessed lighting, range hood and dishwasher connections, and sometimes a panel capacity upgrade. Trident Electric plans these around your new layout before cabinets and drywall go in.

Should electrical work be done before drywall?

Yes. Rough-in wiring, boxes, and circuit routing are installed while walls are open, then inspected where required. Finish work such as devices, fixtures, and trim happens after drywall and paint. Doing rough-in first avoids cutting into finished walls later.

Can remodeling reveal electrical problems?

Yes. Opening walls can expose old or altered wiring, ungrounded circuits, overloaded circuits, aluminum branch wiring, or damaged cable. These should be evaluated and corrected before they are covered again.

Do I need a permit for remodeling electrical work?

Often, yes. Most jurisdictions require an electrical permit and inspection for new circuits, rewiring, and panel changes, while small like-for-like device swaps may be exempt. Requirements vary by city and county, so confirm with your local building department or ask Trident Electric during your estimate.

Will my panel handle a kitchen, bathroom, or basement remodel?

It depends on the panel's capacity, available breaker spaces, and the new loads. Electric ranges, induction cooktops, heated floors, saunas, and added living space can exceed what an older panel can support. A load review answers this before work begins.

Do I need GFCI and AFCI protection in a remodel?

Many remodeled kitchens, bathrooms, basements, and laundry areas now require GFCI and/or AFCI protection under the electrical code adopted locally. The exact requirements depend on the code edition your jurisdiction enforces.

When should I hire an electrician for a remodel?

Early. Involving an electrician during planning, before demolition, lets circuits, lighting, and outlet locations be designed together with the layout and prevents costly changes after walls are closed.

What affects the cost of remodeling electrical work?

The number of new circuits, wiring access, panel capacity, fixture and device count, wet-location requirements, permit and inspection needs, and the age and condition of existing wiring. A written estimate after a walkthrough gives the most accurate number.

Can you add heated floors, baseboard heaters, or a sauna circuit?

Yes. These are higher-load additions that typically need dedicated circuits, correct breaker sizing, and appropriate protection. Trident Electric also handles baseboard and wall heater wiring for finished basements and additions.
