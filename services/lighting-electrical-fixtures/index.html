Direct Answer

What does lighting & electrical fixture service include?

Trident Electric installs, replaces, and repairs indoor and outdoor light fixtures, recessed lighting, chandeliers, ceiling fans, and dimmers and other lighting controls for homes and businesses. That includes the wiring, boxes, switches, and circuit checks behind the fixture, plus testing before the job is closed out.

Best fit: new fixtures, lighting upgrades, fixture replacement, ceiling fans, LED retrofits, or troubleshooting lighting circuits that flicker, buzz, or stop working.

When to Call

Common signs you need lighting or fixture work

Useful starting points, not a substitute for an inspection or diagnosis.

Fixture flickers

Persistent flickering can come from the lamp, fixture, control, wiring, or circuit.

New room needs lighting

A new layout may require circuits, switches, boxes, and fixture connections.

Ceiling fan installation

Fans need a fan-rated box or brace and wiring for the specific model.

Outdoor lighting failure

Outdoor fixtures and wiring need weather-rated equipment and GFCI protection.

Dimmer problems

Incompatible dimmers or lamps cause flicker, buzzing, poor dimming, or heat.

Old fixture replacement

Swapping an outdated fixture can reveal wiring or box issues that need correcting.

Burning smell or warm switch

Turn off the breaker and call an electrician. This can indicate loose connections or overheating.

Lights dim when appliances start

May indicate a shared or overloaded circuit, or a loose neutral connection.

Bulbs burn out repeatedly

Often points to wrong lamp type, enclosed-fixture heat, or a voltage or socket problem.

Troubleshooting

Why do LED lights flicker, buzz, or won't dim?

Most LED dimming problems come from a mismatch between the lamp or fixture, the dimmer, and the load. Common causes:

  • Non-dimmable LED lamp on a dimmer.
  • Dimmer not rated for LED, usually an older incandescent-type dimmer.
  • Load too low for the dimmer's minimum rating.
  • Loose connections at the switch, fixture, or splices.
  • Circuit or neutral problems if many lights flicker together.

What we do

We check compatibility between lamps, drivers, and the dimmer, test the load, inspect connections and boxes, and replace or rewire controls as needed. If flicker follows the whole circuit, we trace the cause rather than just swapping parts.

Safety First

Do not try to DIY hazardous electrical work

A wall switch being off does not make every conductor in a ceiling box safe. Turn off power at the breaker, verify with a tester, and use a qualified professional for electrical installation work.

For electrical faults, new circuits, wet-location work, or concealed wiring, a qualified electrician can evaluate the system and determine the next step.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about lighting & electrical fixtures

How much does it cost to install a light fixture?

National averages published by home-services marketplaces put a typical fixture installation at roughly $160 to $950, with about $550 as a common midpoint. Replacing an existing fixture usually costs less than adding one where no wiring exists. The fixture itself is priced separately, and your electrician should confirm the price after reviewing the box, circuit, ceiling access, and controls.

How long does it take to install a light fixture?

Swapping a standard ceiling-mounted fixture often takes one to two hours. New-location fixtures, recessed lighting, chandeliers on tall ceilings, and outdoor runs take longer because they involve cutting openings, running cable, and adding switches or circuits.

Can you install recessed lights?

Yes. Recessed lighting involves fixture selection, circuit planning, ceiling access, housings (insulation-contact rated where required), controls, wiring, and testing. The exact approach depends on the ceiling type, insulation, and equipment.

How many recessed lights do I need per room?

A common starting point is one light per 4 to 6 square feet, with spacing close to half the ceiling height. Layout depends on fixture beam angle, ceiling height, and whether you also use lamps, pendants, or natural light.

Can a ceiling fan replace an existing light?

Often yes, where a suitable electrical box and circuit arrangement exist. Fans need a box listed for fan support, because a standard light box may not safely carry the weight and motion. The mounting and wiring should be evaluated before installation.

Why do my LED lights flicker or buzz on a dimmer?

The usual causes are an LED lamp or driver that is not dimmable, a dimmer not rated for LED loads, a load that is too low for the dimmer, or loose connections. Matching a compatible dimmer to the lamps or fixture normally solves it. Persistent flicker across many fixtures can point to a circuit problem.

Can you add outdoor lighting?

Yes. Outdoor lighting can involve fixtures, wiring, controls, weather-appropriate equipment, and required protection such as GFCI, and fixtures must be rated for damp or wet locations.

Can you replace an old fixture that has aluminum or damaged wiring?

Replacing a fixture can expose old insulation, loose splices, or an undersized or damaged box. These should be corrected at the same visit, since reconnecting a new fixture to unsafe wiring doesn't fix the underlying problem.

Do I need a permit to install or replace light fixtures?

It depends on the jurisdiction and scope. Like-for-like fixture swaps are often exempt, while new circuits, recessed lighting, and outdoor wiring commonly require a permit. Check local requirements or ask your electrician.

Is it safe to change a light fixture myself?

Flipping a wall switch off does not guarantee every conductor in the ceiling box is de-energized. Shut off the breaker, verify with a tester, and call a qualified electrician if you see damaged wiring, aluminum conductors, scorching, or a box that is not secure.
