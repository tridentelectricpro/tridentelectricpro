What does commercial electrical services include?

Commercial electrical work has to support the way a business operates, from lighting and receptacles to equipment, panels, EV charging, backup power and ongoing repairs. Trident Electric provides commercial electrical services for installations, upgrades, troubleshooting and electrical projects in business properties, tenant spaces, offices, retail areas and facilities.

Best fit: Business properties, tenant spaces, offices, retail areas, facilities and other commercial projects needing electrical installation, repair, upgrade or maintenance work.

Service Scope

Electrical work planned around how your business runs

When power or lighting fails in a business, work stops, so commercial electrical work starts with understanding how the space is used: the equipment, the schedule, the people and the building systems that depend on the electrical service. From there we review the existing panels, circuits and wiring, check what capacity is available, and plan the work around the project and applicable requirements.

Commercial projects often involve more people than a home project: a landlord or property manager, other trades, an inspector and sometimes the utility. Good electrical planning accounts for them early. Equipment lead times, shutdown windows and permit steps are easier to manage when they are discussed at the start. See also multifamily electrical services for apartment and residential-property work.

By Business Type

Electrical needs differ by type of business space

Every space uses power differently. These are common needs by property type; your project may combine several.

Offices & professional spaces

Lighting layouts and controls, receptacle and equipment circuits, panel capacity for added workstations, and wiring changes as teams and floor plans change.

Retail & storefronts

Display and accent lighting, sign and point-of-sale circuits, fixture changes during a refresh, and capacity for new equipment or EV charging for customers.

Restaurants & food service

Dedicated circuits for cooking and refrigeration equipment, GFCI protection where required, and panel capacity when equipment is added or replaced.

Warehouses & light industrial

High-bay lighting, circuits for shop and dock equipment, subpanels, and backup power for loads that cannot go down.

These are general patterns. The building's actual service and the project's code requirements decide what applies.

Commercial EV Charging

Planning EV charging for a business, fleet or property

Charging for customers, employees or a vehicle fleet is a power project as much as an equipment project. The number of chargers and how fast they charge determine how much capacity the building needs, and the available service often decides how many chargers can be installed without an upgrade.

Load management can let several chargers share a limited supply, and parking layout affects conduit routes and trenching. Review EV charger installation for the full scope.

Questions to answer first

  • How many charging ports now and later?
  • Which charging level and speed?
  • Spare capacity in the existing panel and service
  • Distance from the panel to the parking area
  • Landlord, utility and permit requirements
Permits, Inspections & Licensing

Commercial electrical work is regulated

Many commercial electrical projects require a permit and inspection, and requirements depend on the work and the local jurisdiction. In Washington, electrical work is regulated by the Department of Labor & Industries (lni.wa.gov).

Trident Electric is listed with L&I under electrical contractor license TRIDEEL741D4 (UBI 606135927); you can verify the license on the L&I website. Licensing and permit rules differ by state, so confirm the requirements for your project location.

What to settle before work begins

  • Scheduling and any after-hours needs
  • Planned power shutdowns
  • Landlord or building approvals
  • Access to panels and work areas
  • Equipment lead times

Safety First

Do not try to DIY hazardous electrical work

Commercial electrical systems can include higher loads, multiple panels, equipment connections and operational constraints. Do not modify commercial electrical equipment without proper training and authorization.

For electrical faults, panel work, new circuits, high-load equipment, wet-location electrical work or concealed wiring, a qualified electrical professional can evaluate the system and determine the appropriate next step. In an emergency such as fire, call 911.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about commercial electrical services

What commercial electrical work do you provide?

Commercial services include repairs, installations, wiring, panels, lighting, dedicated circuits, EV charging, generators and other electrical upgrades within the project scope.

Can you work during a tenant improvement?

Electrical work can be coordinated with remodeling or tenant-improvement projects, including new circuits, lighting, outlets and equipment connections. Early planning with the landlord, property manager and other trades helps.

Do commercial projects require electrical capacity planning?

Many do, particularly when adding equipment, EV chargers, heating, lighting or other substantial loads. The existing panel, service size and available capacity are checked before new loads are added.

Do commercial electrical projects need permits and inspections?

Many do. New circuits, panel changes and service upgrades commonly require a permit and inspection, and requirements depend on the project and jurisdiction. The Washington State Department of Labor & Industries publishes the state's electrical rules.

What is the difference between single-phase and three-phase power?

Homes almost always use single-phase power. Commercial buildings may use single-phase or three-phase service, and some equipment such as large motors, kitchen equipment or HVAC is built for three-phase. The building's service type affects what equipment and circuits are possible.

Can you coordinate with my landlord or property manager?

Yes. Tenant spaces often need approval for panel access, shutdowns or building-system changes. Share your landlord or property manager contact when you call so those steps can be planned.

What information should I have ready for a commercial project?

The property address and type, the scope of work, a floor plan if you have one, equipment specifications including voltage and phase, photos of the panel or affected area, and your target timeline. Landlord requirements help too.

Do you handle commercial EV charging and generators?

Yes. Commercial EV charging and generator or backup-power electrical work are part of commercial service, including circuits, panel capacity and transfer equipment. See the EV charger and generator service pages for details.
