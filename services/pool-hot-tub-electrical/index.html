Direct answer

What does a pool and hot tub electrician do?

A pool and hot tub electrician installs, repairs, and tests the electrical systems that power pools and spas. That includes dedicated circuits for pumps, heaters, and hot tubs, GFCI protection, equipotential bonding, disconnects, subpanels, and wet-location lighting. Because water raises the shock risk, this work follows NEC Article 680 and is normally permitted and inspected.

Best fit: a new hot tub, a new or renovated pool, a pump or heater upgrade, a light that has failed, or a GFCI that will not stay reset.

Code basics

NEC Article 680 requirements for pools and spas

Article 680 is the part of the National Electrical Code that covers swimming pools, spas, hot tubs, and similar installations. These are the rules homeowners ask about most.

Common Article 680 requirements (summary)
TopicWhat it means in practiceNEC reference
Equipotential bondingLadders, rails, pump motors, reinforcing steel, and light niches are tied together with solid copper, 8 AWG minimum, to reduce voltage differences around the pool.680.26
Pump motor GFCIGFCI protection is required on single-phase 120 to 240V pool pump circuits.680.21(C)
ReceptaclesGenerally at least 6 feet from the pool's inside wall; GFCI protection applies out to 20 feet.680.22
Underwater lightingLuminaires must be listed for the application and protected as required, with proper bonding.680.23
DisconnectsPool equipment disconnects must be in sight and generally at least 5 feet from the pool.680.12
Overhead linesOverhead conductors must keep required clearances above and around the pool.680.9
Spas and hot tubsDedicated circuit, GFCI protection, a disconnect in sight, and bonding as the spa type and location require.680.40 to 680.44

Note: This is a plain-language summary, not a code citation or a substitute for inspection. Your jurisdiction may adopt a different NEC edition or add local amendments, and your inspector has the final say.

Hot tub electrical

How a hot tub circuit is installed

Most hot tub projects follow the same path. The spa's nameplate sets the voltage, amperage, and wire size, so we start there.

  1. Confirm the load. We read the nameplate and installation manual for voltage, amperage, and whether a neutral is needed.
  2. Check panel capacity. A 50 or 60 amp, 240V circuit is a large load, so we confirm the panel can carry it.
  3. Route the circuit. Conduit and wire go from the panel to the spa location, underground or on the surface as site and code allow.
  4. Add the disconnect and GFCI protection. Both are placed to meet code and manufacturer requirements.
  5. Connect, test, and inspect. We make the spa connection, test the GFCI, and coordinate the inspection.

Before the spa arrives

  • Spa make, model, and nameplate
  • Planned location and pad
  • Distance from the panel
  • Open breaker spaces in the panel
  • Any existing pool or spa wiring

Scheduling the electrician before delivery avoids a spa sitting unused while permits and materials catch up.

When to call

Signs you need a pool or hot tub electrician

These are starting points, not a diagnosis. If anything feels unsafe, stay out of the water and call.

GFCI keeps tripping

Repeated trips point to leakage, moisture, a faulty pump or heater, damaged wiring, or a worn protection device.

Adding a hot tub

A new spa usually needs a dedicated circuit, a disconnect, and GFCI protection sized to its nameplate.

Pool light failure

A dark light can mean a failed lamp, a bad connection, a transformer problem, or water in the fixture.

Pump or heater issues

Tripped breakers, humming, or intermittent power can come from the equipment or the circuit feeding it.

Tingling or shocks

Any tingle in or near the water is an emergency. Exit, keep others out, and have the system inspected.

Upgrading equipment

New pumps, heaters, salt systems, or automation can change circuit size, bonding, and protection needs.

Safety first

Do not DIY pool or hot tub wiring

Never touch pool or spa electrical equipment while wet, or when you are unsure whether power is on. Water and electricity create a serious shock hazard.

If you feel a tingle in the water: get out, keep everyone out, switch off power at the breaker only if you can do so without standing in water, and call an electrician before using the pool or spa again.

FAQ

Pool and hot tub electrical: frequently asked questions

Does a hot tub need a dedicated circuit?

Yes, in almost every case. Most 240V hot tubs need their own dedicated, GFCI-protected circuit and a nearby disconnect, sized to the spa's nameplate. Even plug-in 120V models should follow the manufacturer's instructions for a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit.

What size breaker does a hot tub need?

It depends on the nameplate. Many 240V hot tubs use a circuit in the 40 to 60 amp range, but the breaker, wire size, and GFCI protection must match the specific spa and its installation manual. An electrician confirms this before any wiring starts.

Why does pool equipment keep tripping the GFCI?

Common causes include moisture in a connection, a failing pump or heater element, leakage in damaged wiring, or a worn GFCI device. Repeated trips mean current is leaking somewhere, so the equipment should be diagnosed rather than simply reset.

Do I need a permit to wire a pool or hot tub?

Usually yes. Most jurisdictions require an electrical permit and inspection for new pool and spa wiring, and some require a licensed electrician to pull it. Requirements vary by city and county, so we confirm local rules before the work begins.

What is pool bonding and why does it matter?

Equipotential bonding connects the metal parts around a pool, such as ladders, rails, the pump motor, and light niches, with a solid copper conductor (8 AWG minimum under NEC 680.26). It reduces voltage differences in and around the water.

How far must outlets and disconnects be from a pool?

Under NEC Article 680, general receptacles are typically kept at least 6 feet from the pool's inside wall, with GFCI protection out to 20 feet. Pool equipment disconnects must be in sight and generally at least 5 feet from the pool. Your local adopted code governs.

Can you install or repair pool lighting?

Yes. Pool lighting work includes wiring, transformers or drivers, niche and fixture connections, GFCI protection, bonding, and testing. Underwater luminaires must be listed for the application and installed as the manufacturer and code require.

Can I install a hot tub circuit myself?

We do not recommend it. Hot tub wiring combines 240V power, water, and outdoor conditions, and many jurisdictions require a licensed electrician and a permit. Mistakes can create shock hazards that are hard to see.

How much does pool or hot tub electrical work cost?

Cost depends on the distance from the panel, trenching or conduit needs, panel capacity, circuit size, number of pieces of equipment, and permit fees. A hot tub hookup is usually far smaller than full pool wiring. Call for an estimate based on your site.

How long does the work take?

A straightforward hot tub circuit is often completed in about a day once materials and permits are ready. New pool wiring is staged across several visits, such as rough-in, bonding inspection, equipment connection, and final testing.

What should I do if I feel a tingle in the water?

Get out of the water right away and keep everyone out. If you can reach the breaker without stepping in water, switch off power to the pool or spa. Do not use it again until a qualified electrician has found and fixed the cause.

Do you wire pool heaters, pumps, and salt systems?

Yes. We connect and troubleshoot the electrical side of pumps, heaters, salt chlorinators, automation controls, and covers, including dedicated circuits, subpanels near the equipment pad, disconnects, and GFCI protection.
