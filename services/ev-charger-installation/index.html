Direct Answer

What does EV charger installation include?

EV charger installation is the electrical work that lets a charger run safely: a load calculation, a dedicated 240V circuit with correctly sized breaker and wire, the charger connection, permit and inspection, and testing. A panel or service upgrade is added only when the existing capacity cannot carry the new load.

Best fit: new EV owners, homes with limited panel capacity, workplaces, and multifamily properties adding charging.

EV charger installation: key facts

  • Standard home setup: a Level 2, 240V circuit on a dedicated breaker.
  • Breaker size: 125% of charger amps, so a 40A charger uses a 50A breaker.
  • Permit: usually required for a new 240V circuit, with inspection.
  • Panel upgrade: not always needed; a load calculation decides.
  • Typical time: often one visit for a straightforward install.

Circuit Sizing

What size circuit does your EV charger need?

Charging is a continuous load, so under the National Electrical Code the breaker is sized at 125% of the charger rating. Final design depends on wire run, conductor, listing and local rules.

Charger ampsPower at 240VBreaker size
16A3.8 kW20A
24A5.8 kW30A
32A7.7 kW40A
40A9.6 kW50A
48A11.5 kW60A

A hardwired charger suits higher amperage and outdoor mounting. A NEMA 14-50 outlet adds flexibility but limits charging to 40A. GFCI protection is required where the code calls for it.

Panel Capacity

Do you need an electrical panel upgrade for an EV charger?

Not always. We run a load calculation on your existing service and look for open breaker space. There are three common outcomes:

  • Capacity available: add the circuit, no upgrade
  • Tight capacity: load management or a subpanel
  • Insufficient service: panel or service upgrade

Older homes and 100A service

Homes with 100A service, full panels or older equipment are the most likely to need extra capacity. See our panel and service upgrade page, and consider surge protection to protect the charger and vehicle electronics.

Permits & Code

Permits, inspections and licensing

A new 240V EV circuit usually requires an electrical permit and inspection. We size the circuit under the National Electrical Code, including Article 625 for EV charging equipment, and coordinate permits with the local authority. In Washington, electrical rules are published by the Department of Labor & Industries. Trident Electric holds license TRIDEEL741D4; you can verify it on L&I. Unpermitted work can create insurance and resale problems.

Safety First

Do not DIY EV charger wiring

EV chargers are substantial continuous loads. Never plug a high-current charger into an unsuitable receptacle or extension cord, and never improvise wiring or protection. Burning smells, hot plugs or breakers that keep tripping are reasons to stop charging and call a professional. In an emergency such as fire, call 911.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about EV charger installation

How much does EV charger installation cost?

Electrical installation for a Level 2 charger commonly runs from about $800 to $2,500 when the panel has capacity and the run is short. Projects needing a panel upgrade, long conduit runs or trenching can reach $2,000 to $6,000 or more. Charger hardware is extra. Trident Electric gives a written scope and price after reviewing your panel and charger location.

Do all EV chargers need a panel upgrade?

No. It depends on the charger load, existing service capacity, other electrical loads, and the installation requirements. A load calculation shows whether your panel can carry the added load, and options such as load management can sometimes avoid a full upgrade.

Can you install a Level 2 home charger?

Yes. The electrical work includes a dedicated 240V circuit, wiring, breaker protection, equipment connection and testing, sized for the charger and the site.

Do I need a permit to install an EV charger?

Usually yes. A new 240V circuit generally needs an electrical permit and inspection. In Washington, electrical work is regulated by the Department of Labor & Industries. Requirements vary by jurisdiction.

How long does installation take?

A straightforward Level 2 installation is often completed in a single visit once permits are in place. Panel upgrades, long runs, trenching or multiple chargers take longer.

What size breaker does a Level 2 charger need?

EV charging is a continuous load, so the breaker is sized at 125% of the charger's amperage: 32A needs a 40A breaker, 40A needs 50A, and 48A needs 60A. Wire size, distance and the charger's listing also affect the design.

Can you install a Tesla Wall Connector or other brands?

Yes. We connect Tesla Wall Connectors and other UL-listed Level 2 chargers, whether you buy the unit yourself or choose one before the electrical work.

Is a 240V outlet or a hardwired charger better?

Hardwired units suit higher amperage and outdoor mounting. A NEMA 14-50 outlet is more flexible for portable chargers but limits charging to 40A. We can advise based on your vehicle and garage.

Can EV chargers be installed for businesses or multifamily properties?

Yes. Electrical planning covers commercial and multifamily charging, including capacity review, multiple circuits and load management for the site.

Can I install an EV charger myself?

We do not recommend it. Chargers are continuous high-current loads, and mistakes in wiring or protection create fire and shock risks. Unpermitted work can also cause insurance and resale problems.
