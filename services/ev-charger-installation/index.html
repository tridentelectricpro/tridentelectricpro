When to Call

Common signs this service may be needed

These are useful starting points, not a substitute for an electrical inspection or diagnosis.

Buying an EV

Planning the electrical work before the charger is installed helps identify circuit and capacity requirements.

Existing charger trips

Repeated trips can indicate a load, circuit, wiring, or equipment problem.

Long charging times

The charger, vehicle, circuit, and available power all affect charging performance.

Panel has limited capacity

An electrical capacity review may be needed before adding a substantial EV load.

Installing multiple chargers

Commercial or multifamily charging can require coordinated load and circuit planning.

Moving a charger

A new location can require different wiring length, routing, protection, and equipment considerations.

Safety First

Do not try to DIY hazardous electrical work

EV chargers are substantial electrical loads. Do not connect a high-current charger to an unsuitable receptacle or circuit, and do not improvise wiring or protection to make a charger work.

For electrical faults, panel work, new circuits, high-load equipment, wet-location electrical work, or concealed wiring, a qualified electrical professional can evaluate the system and determine the appropriate next step.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about ev charger installation

Do all EV chargers need a panel upgrade?

No. It depends on the charger load, existing service capacity, other electrical loads, and the installation requirements.

Can you install a Level 2 home charger?

The electrical portion of a Level 2 installation can include circuit, wiring, protection, equipment connection, and testing as appropriate for the charger and site.

Can EV chargers be installed for businesses or multifamily properties?

Yes, electrical planning can be performed for commercial and multifamily charging projects, with capacity and circuit requirements considered for the site.
