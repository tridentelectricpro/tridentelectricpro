When to Call

Common signs this service may be needed

These are useful starting points, not a substitute for an electrical inspection or diagnosis.

Outlet feels warm

Heat, discoloration, buzzing, or a burning smell around an outlet is a warning sign.

Outlet is loose

A loose receptacle can indicate mounting or connection problems and should not simply be ignored.

GFCI will not reset

A GFCI that trips repeatedly or will not reset may indicate a fault or device problem.

Switch is buzzing

Buzzing, arcing, heat, or unusual operation can indicate a switch or circuit issue.

Need more outlets

Permanent electrical needs are better addressed with properly installed receptacles than with chains of extension cords.

Adding smart controls

Smart switches and dimmers may require specific wiring, neutral conductors, load compatibility, or other conditions.

Safety First

Do not try to DIY hazardous electrical work

Do not replace a device based only on wire color or assumptions about the circuit. A receptacle or switch box can contain multiple circuits or energized conductors.

For electrical faults, panel work, new circuits, high-load equipment, wet-location electrical work, or concealed wiring, a qualified electrical professional can evaluate the system and determine the appropriate next step.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about switches & outlets

Why is an outlet dead when the breaker looks on?

Possible causes include a tripped GFCI upstream, a failed device, a loose or broken connection, or another circuit problem. The circuit should be diagnosed rather than repeatedly replacing the outlet.

Can you install smart switches?

Yes. Smart-switch installation depends on the device, wiring, load, box space, and whether a neutral conductor is present. Compatibility is checked before installation.

Why does my GFCI keep tripping?

Repeated GFCI trips can indicate current leakage, a connected appliance problem, moisture, wiring issues, or a worn device. Repeated resets without finding the cause are not recommended.

Can you move or add an outlet or switch?

Yes. Relocating or adding an outlet or switch usually involves routing new cable, checking circuit capacity, and patching access openings. Difficulty depends on wall construction and how close existing wiring is.

How much does outlet or switch installation cost?

Cost depends on the number of devices, the device type, whether new wiring must be run, access to the wall or attic, and permit requirements. Replacing a single device is a smaller job than adding a new circuit. Call (855) 599-0507 to describe the project.

Do I need a permit to replace or add an outlet?

Requirements vary by jurisdiction. Like-for-like device replacement is often treated differently from new circuits or relocations. Ask your local building department, or have a licensed electrician confirm what applies.

What is the difference between GFCI and AFCI protection?

A GFCI helps protect people from shock by detecting current leaking to ground and is common in wet or damp areas. An AFCI helps detect arcing conditions that can cause fires and is commonly used on bedroom and living-area circuits. Required locations depend on the code edition adopted locally.

My outlets only have two slots. Can they be replaced?

Two-slot outlets often mean there is no equipment ground. Options can include GFCI protection labeled as no equipment ground, or running a grounded circuit. An electrician should evaluate the wiring first.

Should I replace a warm, buzzing, or discolored outlet myself?

No. Heat, buzzing, discoloration, or a burning smell can point to a loose connection or overloaded circuit. Turn off the circuit if it is safe to do so and have a qualified electrician inspect it.
