Planning an Electrical Project

What to have ready when you call

A few details make the first conversation much more useful. Describe what is happening or what you want installed, where it is, and what equipment is involved. If it is a problem, note whether it is constant or intermittent. If it is a project, note any plans for an addition, remodel or new equipment that will add electrical load.

Many electrical projects require a permit and inspection, and requirements depend on the work and the jurisdiction. In Washington, the Department of Labor & Industries publishes the state's electrical rules at lni.wa.gov. Some jobs need an on-site assessment before the scope and price can be set.

Helpful information to provide

  • Property type and location
  • What is happening or being added
  • Which room, circuit, panel or equipment
  • Constant or intermittent problem
  • Equipment model or specifications
  • Photos of the area when it is safe
Electrical Safety

Know when not to wait

Burning smells, smoke, sparks, a hot or buzzing panel, water near electrical equipment or damaged wiring are safety concerns. Avoid opening equipment or attempting repairs yourself, turn off the affected circuit at the breaker only if it is safe to reach, and call a qualified professional. In an emergency such as fire, call 911.
