Quick Answer

What is whole-house surge protection?

Whole-house surge protection is a surge protective device (SPD) wired into your main electrical panel. It diverts short voltage spikes from utility power, lightning, or large appliances to ground, shielding every circuit and everything plugged into it. A licensed electrician typically installs one in one to two hours.

Best fit: Homes and buildings with computers, smart-home gear, HVAC, EV chargers, or other costly electronics.

Why It Matters

Where power surges come from

A surge is a brief spike in voltage, lasting only microseconds, that can degrade or destroy circuit boards. Outside causes include lightning, utility switching, and downed lines. Many surges start inside the building, when motors in HVAC systems, refrigerators, and pumps cycle on and off.

Repeated small surges often do the most harm. They wear down electronics gradually, so devices fail early with no obvious cause. A whole-house SPD limits the spike at the panel, before it reaches your wiring.

Services such as panel upgrades and wiring work best when grounding and bonding are sound, which is why Trident Electric checks them during every SPD install.

Pricing

What affects surge protector installation cost

Labor is usually a large share of the total, so choose a quality device rather than the cheapest one.

SPD type and rating

Higher surge-current ratings and better voltage protection cost more but absorb more events.

Panel condition

Open breaker space, clear access, and a sound panel keep installs simple. Older or full panels may need work first.

Grounding and wiring

Poor grounding limits what an SPD can do. Corrections add time and cost.

Property type

Three-phase commercial and multifamily panels use different equipment than a single-family home.

Bundled work

Adding an SPD during a panel upgrade or new build often lowers extra labor.

Permits and code

Local permit and inspection rules can affect scheduling and fees.

Safety & Code

Leave panel work to a licensed electrician

Service panels hold live conductors even with the main breaker off. Do not open the panel to install an SPD yourself. Recent editions of the National Electrical Code require surge protection at new dwelling-unit services, and many areas require permits for the work.

Surge protection is one layer of protection, not a guarantee. Pair it with sound grounding and point-of-use protection for sensitive electronics.

FAQ

Surge protection questions answered

What is a whole-house surge protector?

A whole-house surge protector, technically a surge protective device (SPD), is installed at or next to your main electrical panel. It diverts short voltage spikes to ground before they travel through your wiring to outlets, appliances, and electronics.

How much does whole-house surge protection installation cost?

Installed prices commonly fall between roughly $300 and $700 for a typical home, depending on the SPD type and rating, panel layout, and labor. Panel upgrades or extra wiring raise the total. Trident Electric confirms a firm price after reviewing your panel.

How long does installation take?

Most panel-mounted SPD installations take about one to two hours once the equipment is on site. Power to the home is shut off briefly while the device is connected.

What is the difference between Type 1, Type 2, and Type 3 surge protectors?

Type 1 SPDs connect on the line side of the main breaker, Type 2 SPDs connect on the load side at the panel, and Type 3 are point-of-use devices such as protected power strips. Most homes use a Type 2 whole-house device, often paired with Type 3 protection for sensitive electronics.

How long does a whole-house surge protector last?

Lifespan depends on how many surges it absorbs. Many last several years, and some quality units last a decade or more. Check the status indicator lights regularly and replace the unit if they show the protection is no longer active.

Is a whole-house surge protector better than a power strip?

They do different jobs. A panel-mounted SPD protects every circuit, including hardwired appliances, HVAC, and EV chargers. A power strip protects only what is plugged into it. Using both gives layered protection.

Does surge protection stop every surge?

No. A direct lightning strike can exceed any device's rating. Surge protection is designed to reduce the impact of common transient events, not to guarantee that no damage will ever occur.

Can surge protection be added to an existing panel?

Usually yes. A compatible SPD can be added when the panel has available space and meets the manufacturer's installation requirements. If the panel is outdated or damaged, an electrician may recommend a panel upgrade first.

Does the electrical code require surge protection?

Recent editions of the National Electrical Code require surge protection at new dwelling-unit services, and many jurisdictions have adopted that rule. Requirements vary by location and code edition, so a licensed electrician should confirm what applies to your property.

Can I install a whole-house surge protector myself?

It is not recommended. The work happens inside the service panel, where live conductors are present, and many jurisdictions require a licensed electrician and a permit. Incorrect installation can cause shock, fire, or void the warranty.

Do you install surge protection for businesses and apartment buildings?

Yes. Trident Electric installs SPDs for residential, commercial, and multifamily properties, including protection for panels that serve HVAC, controls, networking gear, and EV charging.

How do I know if my surge protector has failed?

Most SPDs have indicator lights or an alarm showing protection status. If the light is off, red, or flashing as the manual describes, or the home recently took a major surge, have it inspected and replaced.
