What Counts as an Electrical Emergency

An electrical emergency is a situation where there is a risk to people, property, or the safety of your home or business, or where power is lost in a way that leaves you without essential services. Examples include a burning smell from an outlet, switch, or panel; visible sparking or arcing; a panel that feels hot to the touch; smoke coming from electrical equipment; a breaker that trips repeatedly or will not reset; and a total loss of power that is not explained by a known utility outage. Water coming into contact with electrical equipment, damaged wiring after a storm, and a fallen service line are also emergencies. A single tripped breaker with no smell, heat, or sparking is often less urgent, but it still deserves attention if it keeps happening. If you feel unsafe, treat the situation as an emergency. Do not wait to see whether the problem goes away on its own, because electrical faults can worsen without warning. We would rather hear from you early than after a small problem has become a larger one.

What to Do First — Safety Steps Before an Electrician Arrives

Your first job in an electrical emergency is to keep yourself and everyone else safe. If you see sparking, smoke, or flames, leave the area and call 911 if there is a fire or an immediate danger to life. Do not use water on an electrical fire. If it is safe to do so, turn off power at the main breaker only if you can reach it without stepping in water, touching damaged wiring, or standing near smoke or sparking equipment. If you cannot reach the panel safely, leave it alone and wait for help. Move children and pets away from the affected area, and do not plug anything else into a sparking or hot outlet. If you smell burning, open a window for fresh air if you can do so safely, but do not stay in a room that is filling with smoke. Unplug appliances only if the plug and cord are cool, dry, and undamaged, and never pull a plug by its cord. Note what you observed — when it started, which rooms are affected, and whether you heard buzzing or popping — because that information helps the electrician. Then call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507. Stay on the phone if we ask you to, and do not try to open the panel or repair wiring yourself.

Power Outage — When It Is the Utility vs Your Panel

When the power goes out, it helps to know whether the problem is in your home or on the utility side. First, check whether your neighbors have power. If the whole street is dark, the cause is likely a utility outage, and you should contact your utility provider for updates. If your neighbors have power and your home does not, the problem is more likely inside your home, such as a tripped main breaker, a failed main breaker, or a problem with your service equipment. Check whether some rooms still have power. If only part of your home is out, a single circuit may have tripped, or part of your panel may have lost power. Look at your panel for a breaker that is in the tripped position, but do not reset it more than once, and do not reset it at all if you smell burning, see sparking, or notice heat. If the main breaker trips again right away, leave it off and call us. Keep your refrigerator and freezer closed to preserve food, unplug sensitive electronics to protect them from a surge when power returns, and use flashlights rather than candles. If you are unsure whether the outage is a utility problem or a problem in your home, call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507 and we will help you work through the next steps.

Burning Smell, Sparking, or a Hot Panel

A burning smell, sparking, or a hot electrical panel should always be treated seriously. These are signs that heat is building up somewhere it should not, and that heat can damage wiring, outlets, and equipment. The smell may be faint at first, like hot plastic or dust, or it may be strong and sharp. Sparking may appear as a brief flash when you plug something in, or as repeated arcing at an outlet or switch. A panel that feels warm or hot to the touch, a breaker that is hot, or a buzzing sound from the panel are also warning signs. If you notice any of these, stop using the affected outlet, switch, or circuit. Do not keep resetting a breaker that serves the affected area, and do not cover the smell with air freshener or ignore it in the hope that it will pass. If there is smoke or flames, leave the area and call 911. If it is safe, turn off the affected breaker, but do not open the panel cover or touch wiring inside it. Keep the area clear, note which outlet or circuit is involved, and call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507. An electrician will need to inspect the wiring, connections, and devices on that circuit to find the source of the heat before the circuit is used again.

A Breaker That Will Not Reset

A breaker that will not reset is telling you that something on that circuit is still wrong. Breakers are designed to trip when a circuit is overloaded or when a fault is present, and they will not stay on if the fault has not cleared. First, unplug everything on the affected circuit, including lamps, heaters, and appliances, then try to reset the breaker once by pushing it firmly to the off position and then back to on. If it stays on with everything unplugged, plug items back in one at a time to see whether one of them causes the trip. If the breaker trips again immediately, even with everything unplugged, leave it in the off position and do not force it. Do not tape a breaker in the on position, wedge it, or replace it with a larger breaker, because that removes the protection it provides. A breaker that feels hot, buzzes, or shows signs of damage should also be left off. Repeated tripping can point to a short circuit, a ground fault, damaged wiring, or a failing breaker, and each of those needs an electrician to diagnose. FAQ

Electrical repair questions, answered

When should I call an emergency electrician instead of waiting until morning?

Call right away if you see sparking, smell burning, notice smoke, feel heat at a panel or outlet, or have lost power without a known utility outage. Call 911 first if there is a fire or immediate danger. If a breaker trips once with no smell, heat, or sparking, you can often wait until morning, but call us if it trips again.

What should I do if I smell burning from an outlet or my panel?

Stop using the affected outlet or circuit and do not keep resetting its breaker. If there is smoke or flames, leave the area and call 911. If it is safe, turn off the affected breaker, keep the area clear, and call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507. Do not open the panel or touch wiring inside it.

Is a tripped breaker an emergency?

A single tripped breaker is not always an emergency, especially if there is no smell, heat, or sparking and it resets and stays on. It becomes urgent if it trips repeatedly, will not reset, feels hot, buzzes, or is linked to a burning smell or sparking. In those cases, leave the breaker off and call our 24/7 emergency line.

What if the power is out in only part of my house?

Partial power loss often points to a tripped breaker, a failed breaker, or a problem with one part of your panel or wiring. Check your panel for a tripped breaker, but do not reset it more than once, and do not reset it if you smell burning or see sparking. If the problem persists, call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507.

What information should I have ready when I call the emergency line?

Have your address, a phone number where we can reach you, and a short description of what is happening ready. Note when the problem started, which rooms or circuits are affected, and whether you see sparking, smell burning, or notice heat. Tell us what you have already tried, such as resetting a breaker.

Should I touch the electrical panel myself?

Only turn a breaker off or on if you can reach the panel safely, without stepping in water, touching damaged wiring, or standing near smoke or sparking equipment. Never open the panel cover or touch wiring inside it. If you are unsure, leave the panel alone and call our 24/7 emergency line at (855) 599-0507 for guidance.

Yes. Our emergency line is available 24/7 at (855) 599-0507 for urgent electrical problems. Our office hours for non-emergency work are Monday through Saturday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. If your problem is not urgent, you can call during office hours or use the emergency line to ask whether it needs attention sooner.
