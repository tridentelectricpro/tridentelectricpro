Quick Answer

What is an electrical panel upgrade?

An electrical panel upgrade replaces or enlarges the main breaker panel (and, when needed, the meter base and service entrance) so your property can safely carry more electrical load. Homeowners typically upgrade to 200 amps when a panel is full, outdated, or damaged, or before adding an EV charger, heat pump, addition, or hot tub.

Best fit: aging, undersized, damaged, overloaded, or fuse-based electrical panels and service arrangements.

When to Call

Signs you may need an electrical panel upgrade

These are useful starting points, not a substitute for an electrical inspection or diagnosis.

Frequent breaker trips

Repeated trips can indicate a circuit or load issue that should be diagnosed rather than simply using larger breakers.

Not enough breaker spaces

A full panel limits practical options for adding circuits and may require a planned upgrade.

Fuse box or 60-amp service

Fuse-based and low-capacity services are a leading reason for upgrades and often draw insurer and buyer attention.

Major renovation planned

A remodel or addition can materially change electrical loads and circuit requirements.

New high-load equipment

EV chargers, heat pumps, electric ranges, hot tubs, and baseboard heat may require a capacity review.

Heat, rust, or burning smell

Scorch marks, corrosion, moisture, buzzing, or hot breakers call for prompt professional evaluation.

Older or flagged panel brands

Certain older brands, such as Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco, have documented breaker reliability concerns and are commonly flagged by inspectors.

Flickering or dimming lights

When lights dim as large appliances start, the service or a connection may be under strain.

Safety note: if you smell burning, see sparks, or find a hot or wet panel, turn off power only if it is safe to do so, stay clear, and call emergency services or the utility if there is any fire risk.

Planning Ahead

Panel upgrades for EV chargers, heat pumps, additions, and more

Most upgrades are triggered by a new load. Plan the panel and the equipment together.

EV chargers and electrification

A Level 2 charger is a continuous, high-current load. Depending on your panel, we may add a dedicated circuit, use load management, add a subpanel, or upgrade the service. See EV charger installation.

Additions, ADUs, and remodels

New rooms add lighting, outlets, and often heating. Sizing the panel in the planning stage avoids opening walls twice. See home addition electrical and remodeling electrical.

Heating and appliances

Heat pumps, electric ranges, dryers, and baseboard heaters can push a 100-amp service to its limit. We check demand before recommending equipment.

Backup power and protection

A new panel is the ideal moment to add a generator transfer switch or whole-house surge protection so your electronics and appliances are protected at the source.

Safety First

Do not try to DIY hazardous electrical work

Never remove a panel cover or work inside a service panel as a DIY project. Parts of the panel can remain energized even when individual breakers are switched off. Do not install a larger breaker simply to stop nuisance tripping.

For electrical faults, panel work, new circuits, high-load equipment, wet-location electrical work, or concealed wiring, a qualified electrical professional can evaluate the system and determine the next step.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about electrical panel upgrades

How much does an electrical panel upgrade cost?

Most straightforward residential panel replacements fall roughly between $1,500 and $4,500, based on commonly published 2026 cost guides. Moving the panel, replacing the meter base or service entrance, upgrading to 400 amps, trenching, or utility coordination can raise the total. Trident Electric provides a written quote after reviewing your panel and loads.

How long does a panel upgrade take?

A like-for-like panel replacement is often completed in one working day, with power off for part of that time. Service upgrades that involve the meter, service mast, or utility disconnect and reconnect can take longer because scheduling depends on the utility and the inspector.

Do I need a permit to replace or upgrade an electrical panel?

In most jurisdictions, yes. Panel and service work normally requires an electrical permit and an inspection, and unpermitted work can cause problems with insurance or a future home sale. Requirements vary by city and county, so the permit path is confirmed before work begins.

What is the difference between a 100-amp and a 200-amp panel?

Amperage is the maximum current the service and panel are rated to carry. 100-amp service suits smaller homes with limited electric loads, while 200-amp service is the common choice for modern homes with EV chargers, heat pumps, electric ranges, hot tubs, or additions. A load calculation determines the right size.

What are the signs I need a new electrical panel?

Common signs include frequent breaker trips, no open breaker spaces, a fuse box or 60-amp service, scorch marks, rust or moisture inside the panel, buzzing or a burning smell, flickering lights under load, and panel brands with documented breaker reliability concerns. Planned loads such as an EV charger or addition can also trigger an upgrade.

Do I need a panel upgrade for an EV charger?

Not necessarily. It depends on available capacity, the charger requirements, existing loads, and the electrical service. A load evaluation can determine the appropriate approach, which may be a new dedicated circuit, load management, a subpanel, or a full panel upgrade.

Does a full breaker panel mean I need a new panel?

Not automatically. The correct solution depends on the panel, service, available capacity, project needs, and applicable requirements. Options can include reorganizing circuits, using approved space-saving breakers where permitted, adding a subpanel, or replacing the panel.

Can a panel upgrade support a home addition?

It can. Additions may increase electrical demand and require new circuits or changes to panel capacity and distribution. Planning the panel together with the addition's wiring, heating, and lighting avoids paying for the same work twice.

Can I replace my own electrical panel?

No. Parts of a service panel remain energized even with the main breaker off, and mistakes can cause shock, arc flash, or fire. Panel work should be done by a qualified electrician under a permit and inspected by the local authority.

Will an older fuse box or knob-and-tube wiring need to be replaced?

A fuse box is a common reason to upgrade, and many insurers and buyers prefer a modern breaker panel. Knob-and-tube or other legacy wiring is evaluated separately during the walkthrough because it may call for rewiring in addition to the panel.
