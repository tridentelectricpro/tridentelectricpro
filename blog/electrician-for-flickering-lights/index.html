Electrician for Flickering Lights: Causes, Checks, and When to Call

Flickering lights are often a loose bulb or a dimmer mismatch, but sometimes they point to a loose connection or an overloaded circuit. Here is how to narrow it down safely.

Flickering lights are easy to ignore at first. A bulb blinks now and then, or the kitchen lights dip when the air conditioner starts, and life goes on. Sometimes the cause is simple, such as a loose bulb or a dimmer that does not suit the bulb. Sometimes it points to a loose connection, an overloaded circuit, or a problem with the service coming into your home. Telling the difference matters, because a loose connection can overheat and become a fire risk, while a simple bulb problem is a quick fix. This guide explains the common causes of flickering, shows you how to narrow the problem down safely, and spells out the warning signs that mean you should call an electrician right away. If you ever smell burning, see sparking, or feel heat around a switch or fixture, stop what you are doing and call a licensed electrician.

What Causes Lights to Flicker?

Lights flicker for many reasons, and most of them fall into a few clear categories. The simplest is a loose bulb. A bulb that is not screwed in firmly can lose contact with the socket as it heats and cools, and the light will blink or cut out. Tightening it gently, with the light off and the bulb cool, often solves the problem. Dimmer switches are another common cause. Older dimmers were designed for incandescent bulbs, and many LED bulbs do not work well with them. The result is flickering, buzzing, or a light that will not dim smoothly. A dimmer designed for LED bulbs, paired with bulbs listed as dimmable, usually fixes this. Loose wiring is a more serious cause. A wire that is not secured tightly at a switch, outlet, or fixture can arc as it makes and breaks contact, and that arcing creates heat. This is the kind of flickering that should be checked by an electrician, because a loose connection behind a wall can smoulder without any visible sign. An overloaded circuit can also make lights flicker. When a large appliance such as a microwave, hair dryer, or air conditioner draws a heavy load, the voltage on that circuit can dip briefly, and the lights dim. If the flicker happens every time a particular appliance runs, the circuit may be carrying more than it should. Voltage problems from the utility side can make lights flicker across the whole house. A loose service connection, a failing transformer, or damage to the service cable can all cause voltage to fluctuate. Finally, a failing breaker can cause flickering on the circuit it protects. Breakers wear out over time, and one that is failing may not hold a steady connection.

How to Narrow It Down Safely

The first question to answer is whether the flicker affects one fixture or the whole house. If a single light flickers, the problem is probably in that fixture, its bulb, its switch, or the wiring feeding it. Try swapping the bulb with one from a fixture that works fine. If the flicker follows the bulb, the bulb is the problem. If it stays with the fixture, the fixture or its wiring is suspect. Check whether the bulb is screwed in firmly, and look for a socket that is discoloured or loose. Do this with the light switched off and the bulb cool to the touch. If several lights on the same circuit flicker, the problem is more likely at the switch, the breaker, or a connection shared by those lights. Note whether the flicker lines up with an appliance starting. If the lights dip every time the air conditioner, refrigerator, or washing machine kicks on, the circuit may be overloaded, or the appliance may be drawing more current than it should. Try running the appliance on its own, with other heavy loads switched off, and see whether the flicker changes. If lights throughout the house flicker, the problem may be at the panel or in the service coming into your home. Check whether neighbours are seeing the same thing, and note whether the flicker is worse at certain times of day. Write down what you observe before you call: which lights flicker, when it happens, and what else is running. That detail helps an electrician diagnose the problem much faster. Never open a panel, remove a switch plate, or pull apart a fixture to investigate. If the cause is not a bulb or a dimmer, leave the inspection to a licensed electrician.

Dimmer Switches and Bulb Compatibility

Dimmer problems are among the most common causes of flickering, and they are also among the easiest to fix. The issue is usually a mismatch between the dimmer and the bulb. Older dimmers were built for incandescent bulbs, which dim by reducing the voltage smoothly. LED bulbs work differently, and many of them need a dimmer designed for LED loads. When an LED bulb is paired with an old dimmer, the result is often flickering, buzzing, or a light that jumps between brightness levels instead of dimming smoothly. The fix is to check that the bulb is labelled dimmable and that the dimmer is designed for LED use. If either one is not, replace the part that does not match. Some LED bulbs flicker even with a compatible dimmer if the dimmer is set very low. Many dimmers have a low-end adjustment that sets the minimum brightness, and raising it slightly can stop the flicker. Flickering can also come from a bulb that is not seated properly in the socket, or from a socket that has worn over time and no longer grips the bulb firmly. If you have recessed lights, check whether the flicker affects all of them or just one. All of them flickering together points to the dimmer or the circuit, while one flickering alone points to that fixture. Another common cause is a bulb that is the wrong wattage for the fixture, or a fixture with a loose connection at the socket. If you replace the bulb and the dimmer and the flicker continues, the problem is deeper, and it is time to call an electrician. We can check the dimmer wiring, test the circuit, and confirm whether the fixture itself needs attention, so you are not guessing with parts that may not solve the problem.

Overloaded Circuits and Large Appliances

Every circuit in your home is designed to carry a set amount of current, and when the load on a circuit exceeds that amount, the voltage drops and the lights flicker. This is most noticeable when a large appliance starts up. Motors in air conditioners, refrigerators, washing machines, and well pumps draw a surge of current when they start, and that surge can make the lights dim for a moment. A brief dip when a motor starts is common and not usually a cause for alarm on its own. But if the flicker is strong, lasts more than a second, or happens every time the appliance runs, the circuit may be overloaded. Overloading often happens when too many things share one circuit. A kitchen circuit feeding a microwave, a toaster, and a coffee maker at once, or a bathroom circuit feeding a hair dryer and a space heater, can easily exceed its capacity. Breakers are designed to trip when a circuit is overloaded, so a breaker that trips often is telling you the circuit is carrying too much. Never replace a tripping breaker with a larger one. The breaker size is matched to the wiring, and a larger breaker can let the wires overheat. If the flicker follows a particular appliance, try plugging it into a different circuit and see whether the flicker moves with it. If it does, the appliance itself may be failing. Adding a dedicated circuit for a large appliance is often the right fix, and it is work for a licensed electrician. We can measure the load on your circuits, check the panel for signs of overheating, and add capacity where your home needs it, so the lights stay steady even when appliances are running.

Warning Signs That Mean Call an Electrician Now

Most flickering is a nuisance, but some flickering is a warning that something is wrong and needs attention right away. If you smell burning near a switch, outlet, or fixture, turn off the light and call an electrician immediately. A burning smell means something is overheating, and it should not be ignored. The same applies if a switch plate or outlet cover feels warm or hot to the touch, or if you see scorch marks, discolouration, or melted plastic around a fixture. Sparking is another urgent sign. If you see sparks when you flip a switch or plug something in, stop using that switch or outlet and call an electrician. Flickering that comes with a breaker tripping repeatedly is also urgent. It can mean a short circuit, a ground fault, or a failing breaker, and each of those can become a fire risk if left alone. Buzzing or crackling sounds from a switch, outlet, or panel should be treated the same way. Turn off the affected circuit if you can do so safely, and call for help. Lights that flicker throughout the whole house, especially if the flicker started suddenly after a storm, can point to a problem with the service cable or the utility connection. If the flicker is paired with appliances behaving strangely, such as a refrigerator cycling oddly or electronics resetting, call an electrician promptly. What an Electrician Checks and When to Call

When we come out for flickering lights, we start by listening to what you have observed, then we test the circuits methodically. We check the fixtures first, looking for loose bulbs, worn sockets, and poor connections at the fixture itself. We then check the switches, including dimmers, for loose wiring, worn contacts, or a dimmer that is not compatible with the bulbs in use. At the panel, we inspect the breaker feeding the affected circuit, testing it and checking for heat, corrosion, or a loose connection on the bus bar. We measure the voltage on the circuit, both with the lights on and while a large appliance is running, to see whether the voltage dips under load. A steady voltage with a flickering light points to the fixture or the switch. A voltage that drops sharply under load points to an overloaded circuit or a failing connection. We also check the neutral connection, because a loose neutral can cause lights to flicker and brighten unpredictably, and it can damage appliances. A loose neutral is a serious problem, and it needs to be found and repaired by a licensed electrician. If the flicker affects the whole house, we check the main breaker, the service connections, and the meter base, and we can coordinate with your utility if the problem is on their side. FAQ

Electrical repair questions, answered

Why do my lights flicker when the air conditioner turns on?

Motors draw a surge of current when they start, which can make lights dip briefly. A short, mild dip is common. If the flicker is strong, lasts more than a second, or happens every time, the circuit may be overloaded and should be checked.

Can a loose light bulb cause flickering?

Yes. A bulb that is not screwed in firmly can lose contact as it heats and cools, making the light blink. With the light off and the bulb cool, tighten it gently. If the flicker continues, try a different bulb to rule out a faulty one.

Why do my LED lights flicker on a dimmer switch?

Many older dimmers were made for incandescent bulbs and do not work well with LEDs. Use bulbs labelled dimmable with a dimmer designed for LED loads. If the flicker continues after matching them, an electrician should check the dimmer wiring.

Is flickering in one room different from flickering in the whole house?

Yes. Flickering in one room usually points to a fixture, bulb, or switch in that room. Flickering throughout the house points to the panel, the neutral connection, or the service coming in, and it should be checked promptly by an electrician.

Should I worry if a light switch feels warm?

Yes. A switch or outlet that feels warm or hot, smells like burning, or shows scorch marks should not be used. Turn off the light, avoid using that switch, and call a licensed electrician right away, especially if the warmth comes with flickering.

When should I call an electrician about flickering lights?

Call right away if there is a burning smell, heat, sparking, buzzing, or repeated breaker trips. For mild flickering in one fixture with no warning signs, book a daytime visit. Our office is open Monday through Saturday, 08:00 to 17:00.
